## The Backstory

My private infrastructure lab started as a way to learn by owning the whole system: compute, storage, networking, deployment, monitoring, and recovery.

It gives me a safe environment for experiments that would be inappropriate in a public product, including local language models, self-hosted services, and automation across different kinds of hardware.

## My Philosophy in Action

I treat the lab as an engineering environment rather than a hardware collection. Services are containerised, changes are repeatable, and experiments are judged by how maintainable they remain after the initial excitement.

The lab also lets me compare local and hosted AI options while keeping private data inside a controlled environment.

## The Hard Truths

Owning the full stack means owning every failure. Automation can remove repetitive work, but every new layer also creates another dependency to understand, update, and recover.

The useful lesson has been restraint: the best self-hosted system is not the one with the most components; it is the one I can still explain and restore when something breaks.
